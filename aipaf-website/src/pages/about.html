The Mandate

Africa does not lack projects. It lacks the assurance that they will deliver.

AIPAF has been founded to give the discipline of delivering, assuring, and investigating projects a professional home: a standard that is African in origin and global in credibility.

Why we exist

Project failure is structural, and governance can be strengthened.

When projects succeed in Africa, the gains are real and lasting. When they fail, the cost is rarely only financial. It is the clinic that never opened, the contractor never paid, and the community that stopped believing promises would be kept.

Cost overruns, schedule slippage, abandonment, and disputed completion are common enough across public infrastructure that they are treated in some quarters as an unavoidable feature of doing business. They are not. They are symptoms of weak project governance.

Weak front-end assurance

Decisions to sanction, finance, and proceed are frequently taken without independent scrutiny of whether the project is deliverable to time, budget, and quality.

A thin diagnostic capability

When a project stalls or a dispute arises, there is often no independent, methodologically sound way to establish what went wrong, who bears responsibility, and how much of the loss was avoidable.

No professional standard

The practitioners who do this work operate without a shared body of knowledge, a common code of conduct, or a recognised credential that tells an employer or a court that they are competent to opine.

The opportunity

One body across the lifecycle.

Project assurance is already a recognised global discipline. What is largely missing is an African institution that adapts it to African delivery conditions and builds local capacity to apply it. Project forensics is a category still taking shape, and no established professional body owns it.

AIPAF pairs the two and organises both into a single profession. They share a method, an evidentiary discipline, and a temperament, and both depend on independence from the delivery team. The Institute also certifies delivery, and keeps it firmly distinct from the two independent functions.

Vision and mission

Where we are going, and how we get there.

A continent where every significant project is built to a sound and accountable standard, independently assured before it proceeds, and honestly examined when it fails, upheld by a recognised profession that Africa built for itself.

Our mission. To establish, uphold, and advance the profession of independent project assurance and project forensics, together with the sound delivery practice on which they rest, across Africa by setting standards of competence and conduct, certifying practitioners against them, building capability through education and research, and providing the institutional home through which the discipline speaks with one credible voice.

Why AIPAF

Built for African conditions. Answerable on the continent.

Each value carries an operational obligation. A body whose worth rests on the trust others place in its judgements cannot afford values it does not enforce.

Independence

Our members opine free of the interests of those who deliver, finance, or benefit from a project. Independence is what makes the Institute's opinions worth relying on.

Evidence

Judgements rest on facts gathered and weighed by sound method, not on assertion, reputation, or pressure. What cannot be evidenced is not opined.

Integrity

Members tell the truth as the evidence shows it, including when the truth is unwelcome to a powerful client or a sponsoring authority.

Competence

A credential from the Institute means the holder can actually do the work to standard, and keeps that competence current.

Public interest

Where the interests of a client and the interests of the communities a project serves diverge, our members hold the public interest as the higher duty.

African ownership

The standard is built for African delivery conditions, governed on the continent, and answerable to the people whose projects it concerns.

Objects of the Institute

Every activity traces back to at least one of eight objects.

  1. Define and maintain the body of knowledge, standards of competence, and code of professional conduct for project delivery, assurance, and forensics in Africa.
  2. Certify individuals as competent to practise, and maintain a public register of those certified.
  3. Provide education, training, and continuing professional development.
  4. Advance research into the causes of project failure and the methods of assurance and forensic investigation.
  5. Promote the adoption of independent project assurance by governments, financiers, and project owners.
  6. Provide a recognised, impartial source of expertise for the investigation and resolution of project failures and disputes.
  7. Uphold the standing of the profession by disciplining members who fall below its standards.
  8. Represent the profession in dialogue with governments, regulators, professional bodies, and international institutions.

Continental ambition

Pan-African by design, built outward from a credible base.

A continental body can set one standard, certify against it consistently, and give a practitioner qualified in one jurisdiction recognition in others. That portability is part of what turns scattered expertise into a profession.

The Institute will be built outward from a credible operating base rather than declared continental from the outset. Early activity will concentrate in Ghana and the surrounding region, with a governance structure and membership framework designed to admit practitioners and chapters across the continent as the body matures.
