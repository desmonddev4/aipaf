One Discipline, Three Domains

The lifecycle of a project, held to one standard.

A project is delivered, assured at its critical decision points, and, when it fails, investigated. The Institute certifies all three, and keeps them apart.

Deliver

Build the project properly.

The practice of planning, controlling, and completing a project to time, cost, quality, and intended benefit. Delivery work under the Institute's standard is governance-ready and auditable from the outset, so that it can withstand independent assurance and, if it comes to it, forensic scrutiny.

Certified through the CPMP

  • Project planning, scheduling, and cost control to a defined standard.
  • Scope, risk, and stakeholder management across the project lifecycle.
  • Governance-ready record-keeping and the evidential trail that makes a project auditable.
  • Completion, handover, and the realisation of intended benefit.

Assure

Protect the value before it is lost.

Independent, forward-looking review of a project's likely performance, for those who sanction, finance, insure, or own it. The question is confidence: can the project as planned and as progressing realistically deliver, and what must change if the answer is in doubt?

Certified through the CPAP

  • Front-end and gateway reviews at critical decision points, testing whether a project is ready to proceed to the next stage.
  • Deliverability and delivery-confidence assessment across scope, schedule, cost, and benefit.
  • Independent review of project governance, controls, procurement integrity, and risk management.
  • Health checks of projects already in delivery, and independent advice to boards, funders, and sponsors.

Investigate

Establish the truth when a project fails.

Independent, backward-looking investigation of a project that has failed, stalled, been abandoned, overrun, or fallen into dispute. The standard is evidence capable of surviving scrutiny: what happened, why, who bears responsibility, and what the avoidable loss amounts to.

Certified through the CPFE

  • Root-cause analysis of project failure, delay, and cost overrun.
  • Forensic delay analysis and quantum assessment for disputes and claims.
  • Investigation of procurement irregularity, fraud, and value leakage in project delivery.
  • Expert evidence and independent expert determination in project disputes and inquiries.
  • Post-project review that converts the findings of failure into lessons for future assurance.

How the domains connect

Failure teaches. Assurance prevents. Sound delivery removes it at source.

Every forensic investigation produces evidence about why projects fail. That evidence is the raw material of better assurance and better delivery alike. The Institute treats this feedback loop as a defining feature of the profession.

The independence firewall

A member may not provide independent assurance or forensic services on a project they delivered, or in which they hold a delivery interest. The separation is written into the code of conduct. Delivery competence and independent scrutiny are certified by the same body but never exercised on the same project by the same member.
