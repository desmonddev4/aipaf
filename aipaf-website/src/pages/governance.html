Governance and Standards

A body that certifies others is governed to at least the standard it demands of them.

Independence, discipline, and public standing rest on a structure that separates those who set standards from those who are judged against them.

Governance structure

Three bodies, each with a distinct job.

The General Assembly

The membership in general meeting is the Institute's ultimate authority. It elects the Governing Council, receives the annual report and accounts, adopts and amends the constitution, and holds the Council to account.

The Governing Council

The Institute's board. It sets strategy, oversees finances and management, and carries ultimate responsibility for the standards the Institute upholds. It is elected by the membership and chaired by an elected President.

The Secretariat

Led by a Chief Executive or Registrar appointed by the Council, it runs the Institute day to day: examinations, the register, education and CPD, and operations. It executes; it does not set the standards it administers.

Standing committees

Separate hands let the Institute certify credibly and discipline fairly.

Standards and Ethics

Owns the body of knowledge, the technical standards, and the code of professional conduct, and keeps them current.

Education and Examinations

Owns the syllabus, the examinations, and the quality of assessment across all routes and tracks.

Membership and Credentials

Governs admission, grading, certification, CPD compliance, and the integrity of the public register.

Professional Conduct and Disciplinary

Investigates and adjudicates complaints against members, at arm's length from the Council, with a separate appeals route.

Research and Publications

Directs the Institute's research into project failure, assurance, and forensic method, and oversees what it publishes.

Finance and Audit

Oversees the Institute's finances and controls, and its own accountability to the standards it asks of others.

Independence safeguards

The Institute's value rests entirely on trust in its impartiality.

Three safeguards are built into its governance from the outset.

  • Separation of powersThose who set standards, those who certify against them, and those who discipline breaches sit in distinct committees with distinct memberships.
  • Conflict-of-interest disciplineCouncil and committee members declare interests and recuse themselves from any matter in which they are conflicted, under a published policy.
  • TransparencyThe register of members, the code of conduct, the standards, and the outcomes of disciplinary process are matters of public record, subject only to due process and fairness to individuals.

Code of professional conduct

What it means to practise under the Institute's name.

The code binds every member. Membership is conditional on adherence to it and to the Institute's technical standards, which set out how assurance and forensic work should be planned, conducted, evidenced, and reported.

  • Act with independence, and decline or withdraw from work where independence cannot be maintained.
  • Opine only on what the evidence supports, and state honestly the limits of that evidence.
  • Hold the public interest above private advantage where the two conflict.
  • Maintain competence, and practise only within it.
  • Handle confidential and sensitive information with the care it demands.
  • Bring no discredit on the profession by conduct inside or outside professional work.

Discipline

A credential is only as strong as the willingness to withdraw it.

The Institute investigates complaints through the Professional Conduct and Disciplinary Committee, under a published procedure that guarantees a fair hearing and a right of appeal. Sanctions range from advice and reprimand to suspension and, in the gravest cases, removal from the register and loss of the right to practise under the Institute's designations. Outcomes are, subject to fairness, a matter of record.

As membership grows in a country or region, the Institute will establish chapters to deliver CPD, convene members, and adapt engagement to national procurement and legal contexts, all within one continental standard. The certification, the code, and the register remain continental and central.

Establishment roadmap

The standard and the governance come first, then the credential, then scale.

  1. Phase One

    Foundation

    • Incorporate the Institute and adopt its constitution and governing instruments.
    • Constitute the interim Governing Council and the core committees.
    • Publish the body of knowledge, the technical standards, and the code of professional conduct.
    • Design the three-track credential, the syllabus, and the examination framework.

  2. Phase Two

    Credential and founding cohort

    • Open founding membership and admit a first cohort by assessed experience and examination.
    • Deliver the first examinations for CPMP, CPAP, and CPFE.
    • Establish the public register and the CPD framework.
    • Begin the research and publication programme.

  3. Phase Three

    Recognition and reach

    • Pursue formal recognition and partnership with governments, financiers, and cognate professional bodies.
    • Pursue statutory recognition and chartering authority, which unlocks the Chartered designations.
    • Promote independent project assurance in public procurement and project finance.
    • Establish the first continental chapters.

The Institute is a not-for-profit professional body, funded by membership subscriptions, examination and certification fees, accredited education and CPD, research and publication, and partnership. Surpluses are applied to its objects, not distributed.
