The second certification stream

Independent scrutiny of the project itself.

AIPAF's project and asset certification framework is designed to give sponsors, lenders, owners, and receivers an evidence-based assessment against a published scheme—not only a credential for the practitioner advising them.

Four proposed schemes

Different project stages need different standards.

Each scheme defines its own scope and assessment. A certificate is tied to the named scheme, defined scope, and date shown on it.

In delivery

Project Assurance Certification

Assesses matters such as baseline integrity, records, change control, forecasting, and risk.

For: sponsors, boards, and lenders.

Review: to the next surveillance point.

Completed asset

Asset Certification

Assesses the as-built position and the basis of the final account.

For: purchasers, lenders, and public owners.

Review: for a fixed period from issue.

Stalled or abandoned

Revival Assurance Certification

Assesses recoverability, residual scope, and conditions for a possible restart.

For: ministries, lenders, receivers, and incoming contractors.

Review: for a fixed period or until material change.

Across a portfolio

Portfolio and Programme Assurance

Assesses an owner's or sponsor's assurance regime across multiple projects.

For: funds, agencies, and sponsors.

Review: to the next surveillance point.

Revival Assurance has a defined boundary. It assesses whether a stalled project may be recoverable and what conditions a restart would require. It does not decide whether restarting is a sound investment, or apportion blame for why the project stalled.

The founding profile also proposes an Abandoned Projects Observatory to study stalled infrastructure and publish its methodology. It is presented as a planned research initiative, not as a live register or service.

What a certificate means

A bounded statement, not a guarantee.

A certificate records conformity with a named scheme, within a defined scope, as at a stated date. It is granted or refused against a published standard; projects are not ranked or scored.

  • Scope matters. A certificate covering only part of a project says so; the register should carry the same scope.
  • It is not a warranty. A certificate does not guarantee outcomes or warrant design adequacy, structural safety, or regulatory compliance.
  • It is kept current. Scheme-specific surveillance and notification of material changes inform whether certification continues.
  • Status is transparent. Reduction, suspension, and withdrawal are recorded under the scheme's published rules and may be appealed.

The public record

Two registers. Clear standing.

The framework calls for public registers that make current and ended certifications checkable, without exposing information that is not needed to verify standing.

Register of Professionals

Membership grade, designation, listed competences, current status, and published disciplinary findings.

Proposed reference: AIPAF-P

Register of Projects and Assets

Certified project or asset, scheme, scope, dates, and current or former certificate status.

Proposed reference: AIPAF-A

Personal contact details, examination records, and assessors' reports are not public-register information. How long ended entries remain visible is to be governed by the published scheme.

Certificate verification is currently provided for professional credentials. Project and asset register availability should be confirmed with the Secretariat.

Expert Panel

The Institute assesses competence; clients instruct the expert.

Under the framework, forensic work is to be undertaken by assessed members instructed directly by their clients. The Institute's role is to assess and list competence, not to direct an expert's opinion.

  • The Institute does not nominate, recommend, or rank panel members, and does not endorse the opinion an expert reaches.
  • Clients instruct panel members directly; the Institute takes no fee or commission from an engagement.
  • The Institute does not accept forensic or consultancy instructions on matters within the scope of its certification.
  • Delivery and independent scrutiny are kept separate: a member must not independently assess a project in which they have a delivery interest.
