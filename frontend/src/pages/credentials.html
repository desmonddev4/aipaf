The Credentials

Three certified designations. One shared standard.

Each designation is earned through a shared professional foundation, examined track papers, and verified practical experience. The three are complementary, not a hierarchy.

The three tracks

Investigate, assure, or deliver.

Most members will hold one designation. A member who holds across the lifecycle is recognised by the Institute as a complete project professional.

The independence firewall

A member may not provide independent assurance or forensic services on a project they delivered, or in which they hold a delivery interest. This separation is written into the code of conduct.

The shared foundation

Every certified member shares a base of knowledge and a common ethical grounding.

Principles of Project Delivery

The lifecycle, governance, financing, procurement, and controls of projects, with an emphasis on African delivery conditions.

Evidence and Method

The gathering, handling, and weighing of evidence to a standard that withstands independent scrutiny, common to both assurance and forensic work.

Professional Ethics and Independence

The code of conduct, the duties of independence and public interest, and the reasoning required to navigate conflicts. Mandatory, and it cannot be waived by any entry route.

Entry routes

Three routes, one standard.

Competence is built in more than one way. No route bypasses the foundation examination or the ethics component.

Progression and maintenance

Certification is a beginning, not an endpoint.

Credentials are held for as long as the holder maintains them, and no longer.

  • Continuing professional developmentEvery certified member completes a minimum annual quantum of assessed CPD. A credential unsupported by current competence lapses.
  • A natural entry pointMany members will enter as delivery practitioners through the CPMP, then specialise into assurance or forensics. The delivery credential is a foundation to build on, not a ceiling.
  • Additional certificationA member may qualify for another designation by completing the track-specific examinations. Holding the delivery credential does not entitle a member to assure or investigate their own delivery work.
  • Advancement to FellowshipSustained distinguished practice, contribution to the profession, or leadership of significant engagements may, on assessment, lead to election as a Fellow.

The Pathway

From Certified to Chartered.

Designations are conferred in two tiers. Certified is the qualification to practise. Chartered is the senior standing above it, reserved for members of sustained experience and distinction.

Certified

Earned by examination

The qualification to practise, earned through the shared foundation, the track examinations, and verified experience. CPMP, CPAP, or CPFE.

Chartered

Senior standing

Conferred on members of sustained distinction once the Institute holds the necessary chartering authority.

Chartered is earned, not self-awarded.

In the Commonwealth and Ghanaian tradition, Chartered signals recognition conferred by statute or formal charter. The Institute will confer Chartered designations only once it holds the necessary chartering authority. Until then it certifies; it does not charter. Progression will rest on sustained senior practice, a record of significant engagements, CPD maintained without lapse, and unbroken adherence to the code of conduct.

Grade and designation are distinct. Grade, from Graduate to Fellow, describes a member's standing within the Institute. The designation, Certified or in time Chartered, describes the professional qualification the member holds.
